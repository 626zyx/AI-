# Compliance Policy for New Employees

## Data Privacy

All employees must complete data privacy training within 14 days of their start date. The training is available at lms.internal.example.com/privacy.

Key obligations:
- Do not share customer data with unauthorized third parties.
- Anonymize or aggregate data before using it in presentations, reports, or public documents.
- Report any suspected data breach immediately to privacy@example.com.
- PII (Personally Identifiable Information) must be stored only on encrypted, company-approved systems.

## Information Security

- All devices accessing corporate resources must have disk encryption enabled and an active screen lock.
- Passwords must be at least 16 characters. Use 1Password Teams for password management.
- Multi-factor authentication is required for all corporate accounts without exception.
- Never share your credentials, SSH keys, or API tokens with anyone, including colleagues.
- Phishing simulations run monthly. Repeated failures trigger mandatory remedial training.

## Intellectual Property

- All work product created during employment belongs to the company as specified in the employment agreement.
- Open-source contributions require manager and legal approval through the OSS approval process.
- Do not upload proprietary code, documents, or data to personal accounts, public repositories, or unapproved third-party services.

## Workplace Conduct

- Review and acknowledge the Code of Conduct at policy.internal.example.com/conduct within 14 days.
- Harassment, discrimination, and retaliation are prohibited. Report concerns to HR or the anonymous ethics hotline at ethics.internal.example.com.
- Conflicts of interest must be disclosed to your manager and HR.

## Confidential Information

Confidential information includes but is not limited to:
- Employee performance data, salary information, and personnel records
- Unannounced product features and release timelines
- Financial results before public earnings release
- Merger, acquisition, and strategic partnership discussions
- Security incident details and vulnerability reports
- Attorney-client privileged communications and legal strategy
- Customer contracts, pricing, and personally identifiable information

Sharing confidential information outside authorized audiences is a violation of company policy and may result in disciplinary action up to and including termination.
