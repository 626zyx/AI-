# Data Compliance and Privacy for Onboarding

## Overview

This document summarizes key data compliance obligations that affect your day-to-day work. It supplements the full compliance policy available at policy.internal.example.com/compliance. All employees must acknowledge this document within 14 days of their start date.

## Data Protection Principles

1. **Lawfulness, Fairness, and Transparency:** Process personal data only for specified, legitimate purposes. Be transparent about what data you collect and why.
2. **Purpose Limitation:** Do not use data collected for one purpose for an unrelated purpose without additional consent or legal basis.
3. **Data Minimization:** Collect only the data you need. Do not hoard data "just in case."
4. **Accuracy:** Keep personal data accurate and up to date. Correct inaccuracies promptly.
5. **Storage Limitation:** Do not keep personal data longer than necessary. Follow the data retention schedule at policy.internal.example.com/retention.
6. **Integrity and Confidentiality:** Protect data with appropriate security measures. Encrypt data at rest and in transit.

## Customer Data Handling

- Customer PII includes: names, email addresses, phone numbers, physical addresses, tax identification numbers, payment information, and any data that can identify an individual.
- Never export customer PII to personal devices, personal cloud storage, or unapproved third-party services.
- When sharing customer data internally, use only approved channels: Google Drive (Shared Drives), ownCloud, or internal GitLab repositories.
- Anonymize or aggregate customer data before using it in presentations, reports, or analytics dashboards shared broadly.

## Internal Data Classification

- **Public:** Can be shared with anyone. Examples: blog posts, press releases, job listings.
- **Internal:** For employees only. Examples: team wikis, project plans, meeting notes.
- **Confidential:** Limited to those with a business need. Examples: performance reviews, compensation data, security incidents, financial results.
- **Restricted:** Highest sensitivity. Examples: customer PII, legal privileged communications, M&A documents, source code for unreleased products.

## Incident Reporting

If you suspect a data breach or privacy violation:
1. Immediately notify privacy@example.com and security@example.com.
2. Preserve all relevant evidence (do not delete or modify files).
3. Do not discuss the incident with anyone outside the investigation team.
4. Document what happened, when you discovered it, and what data may have been affected.

## Cross-Border Data Transfers

- Some countries have restrictions on transferring personal data across borders. Before sending data internationally, check with the legal team at legal@example.com.
- Use only company-approved data transfer mechanisms (standard contractual clauses, binding corporate rules).

## Retention and Disposal

- Follow the data retention schedule strictly. Do not keep data beyond its retention period.
- When disposing of data, use secure deletion methods. For digital files, use the company-approved shredding tool.
- Physical documents containing Internal, Confidential, or Restricted data must be shredded using cross-cut shredders.
